Micro Wedding Decor Cost in Charlotte: What You’re Actually Paying For

One of the first questions couples ask us about a micro wedding is simple: what will the decor actually cost? It's a fair question, and the honest answer starts with a shift in thinking. With fewer guests, you aren't paying less per detail. You're choosing which details matter and doing them well. A small guest list is what makes a more designed look affordable in the first place.

Here's what drives the cost of micro wedding decor in Charlotte, what you can expect to get at different levels, and how to set a budget that fits.

What a “micro wedding” usually means

Most micro weddings are celebrations of roughly 10 to 50 guests, and many are closer to 20. That size changes the decor math. You don't need a ballroom's worth of centerpieces or a wall of ceremony seating, so the budget can go into quality instead of quantity.

The five things that move the price

  • The ceremony focal piece. A floral arch, installation, or statement arrangement is usually the largest single item, because it's custom work and the most photographed.
  • Reception tables. Linens, place settings, glassware, candles, and centerpieces add up quickly, even for two or three tables.
  • Furniture and rentals. Tables and chairs are a separate cost if your venue or yard doesn't provide them.
  • Florals themselves. Seasonal flowers cost less and look better than out-of-season ones that have to be shipped in.
  • Delivery, setup, and teardown. Someone has to bring it, build it, and take it away. When this is included, you're paying for your own day to go smoothly.

What it looks like at two levels

To make this concrete, here's how we structure our own micro wedding decor for celebrations of up to about 20 guests.

A focused package built around the moments that matter most:

  • a design consultation with a custom color palette and concept direction,
  • one statement ceremony floral arrangement or installation,
  • styled reception tables for up to 20 guests, with centerpieces, candles, and curated details,
  • a custom welcome sign with a floral accent,
  • delivery and professional setup.

Full Bloom: $3,500

A complete styling experience for up to 20 guests, for couples who don't want to coordinate rentals separately:

  • everything in a design consultation and a statement floral arch with seasonal flowers and greenery,
  • reception tables and chairs, premium linens, and full table settings with plates, glassware, flatware, and napkins,
  • centerpieces and candles, plus a styled cake table,
  • a custom welcome sign, and delivery, setup, and breakdown.

The difference between the two is mostly scope: the first styles the key moments, and the second builds the whole reception. You can see both on our micro wedding decor page. Larger groups, custom installations, and different venues are quoted individually.

Where couples save without losing the look

  • Invest in one standout piece and keep everything else simple and cohesive.
  • Choose seasonal flowers and let your designer substitute where it makes sense.
  • Use what your venue provides for furniture, and style over it instead of replacing it.
  • Skip what guests won't notice, such as elaborate chair decor or oversized centerpieces that block conversation.

Where it's worth spending

Spend where the photos and the feeling of the day come from: the ceremony backdrop, the lighting, and the table styling. These are what you'll remember and what appears in every picture. Cutting corners on setup and teardown is usually a mistake too, because someone you love ends up doing it during or after your wedding.

How to set your decor budget

  1. Decide your guest count and venue first, because they determine what you need to bring in.
  2. List what the venue already includes: furniture, linens, lighting, and power.
  3. Choose your one or two priorities, usually the ceremony piece and the tables.
  4. Ask every vendor what's included: delivery, setup, teardown, and any rentals.
  5. Leave a small buffer for the details that always appear late.

The bottom line

A micro wedding gives you something rare: the chance to design a day that's detailed and personal without a large guest list's cost. Decide what matters, put your budget there, and keep the rest simple.
